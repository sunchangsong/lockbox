# How do we use LockBox to protect course content in our education and training business?

As an education and training institution, our core assets are not classrooms, equipment or promotional pages, but the course content accumulated over a long period of time.

Behind a set of mature courses, it often includes the polishing of the teaching and research team, the experience of the lecturers, the collection of real cases, the design of courseware materials, and the continuous investment in subsequent student services. Whether it is vocational skills training, exam coaching, internal corporate training, or online training camps and paid knowledge courses, course videos and supporting materials are the basis for the continuous delivery and reuse of our business.

But in actual operations, we quickly discovered a problem: once a course is sent to students using ordinary video files, network disk links, or chat tools, it is difficult to continue to control its spread.

Students may forward videos to friends, corporate customers may copy internal training packages to more unauthorized personnel, and channel partners may continue to retain course materials after the authorization period expires. For training institutions, these situations will directly affect course revenue, brand reputation, student fairness and content asset value.

Therefore, what we need is not just a player, but a content protection solution that allows safe delivery of courses, controllable authorization, and stable learning experience. LockBox solves exactly this problem.

## Course delivery issues we encountered before using LockBox

### 1. Ordinary video files are too easy to copy

In the past, we used to package course videos and send them to students, or provide them for download through network disks, group files, or internal systems.

This method seems convenient, but as long as the file is downloaded locally, it is almost out of control. Students can copy it to a USB flash drive, forward it to other devices, upload it to a network disk, or continue to spread it in the community.

For higher-priced system courses, skills training courses and enterprise customized courses, this naked file delivery method is very risky.

### 2. Only prevent downloading but not screen recording

Later we also tried online playback and restricted download links. But the actual situation is that as long as the course can be played, it may still be ripped by screen recording tools, virtual machine environments or capture software.

Many pirated courses are not due to server file leaks, but are recorded on the playback end and then uploaded and sold again.

This made us realize that the protection of education and training content cannot just stop at the file level, but also considers the security of the playback end.

### 3. Different courses require different authorization rules

Our course types are not one-size-fits-all:- Some courses are 30-day training camps
- Some courses are valid for 6 months
- Some courses are annual internal training of the company
- Some courses are only available to designated employees.
- Some courses need to be delivered offline to off-line students
- Some courses come with PDF handouts and case materials

Without an authorization management system, these rules can only rely on manual communication and table recording, which is prone to errors and difficult to manage on a large scale.

### 4. Corporate customers are more concerned about whether data is leaked

In corporate training projects, clients often provide internal processes, business cases, product information or job operating specifications.

The content is sometimes more sensitive than public courses. Customers are not only concerned about whether employees can learn smoothly, but also whether course materials will be continued to be disseminated by resigned employees, outsourced personnel or unauthorized departments.

If such projects are still delivered with ordinary documents, it will be difficult to reassure customers.

Through LockBox, we can set the authorization period, playback rules and device range according to the course type, making course delivery closer to the real business model.Course permissions are also easier to manage when courses expire, corporate projects end, employees leave, or partner authorization is terminated.
