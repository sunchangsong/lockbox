## Why does corporate confidential information easily get out of control?

### 1. The cost of copying ordinary files is too low

Whether it is a PDF, video, picture, document or compressed package, as long as you send it to the other party in the form of an ordinary file, it can be easily copied to a USB flash drive, uploaded to a network drive, forwarded to a chat group, or saved to a personal computer.

It is difficult for companies to know how many copies of files have been copied, and it is also difficult to determine where the data ends up.

This uncontrollable dissemination poses a high risk for sales policies, product quotations, technical documentation, customer solutions and internal training content.

### 2. Access rights often fail to keep up with personnel changes

Internal personnel, outsourcing teams, agents, distributors, suppliers and project partners will all be exposed to different types of data.

But in reality, personnel relationships are constantly changing:

- Employees will leave or change jobs
- Outsourcing projects will end
- Agent cooperation will expire
- Customer projects will be delivered and completed
- The temporary team will be disbanded
- Channel policy will be updated

If the information has been sent out in the form of ordinary files, it will be very difficult to revoke the permissions later.

### 3. Only restricting downloads cannot truly prevent leakage

Many companies think that as long as they are not open for downloading, the data is safe.

But as long as the content can be opened, it may be screenshotted, recorded, rephotographed, or collected through a virtual environment. Especially content such as training videos, demonstration materials, product descriptions, and solution explanations can easily be saved twice during playback or reading.

This shows that the protection of corporate confidential data cannot only stop at the transmission link, but also pay attention to the security during opening and use.

### 4. External cooperation data is more difficult to manage

Enterprises often need to send information to external parties, such as:

- Dealer product information
- Agent training content
- Franchisee Operation Manual
- Supplier technical specifications
- 客户项目交付文档
- Bidding and business information
- After-sales service process

These objects are not in the enterprise's internal management system, and permission boundaries are more difficult to control. Once data is saved, copied or forwarded, it is often difficult for companies to track and restrict it.

### 5. Post-breach remediation costs are high

After confidential information is leaked, companies often need to invest a lot of time in dealing with follow-up issues: communicating with customers, investigating sources, adjusting quotations, updating plans, retraining, legal evidence collection, and even bearing brand and business losses.

Compared with subsequent remediation, establishing a data encryption and authorization management mechanism in advance is cheaper and more controllable.
